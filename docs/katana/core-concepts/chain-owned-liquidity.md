# What Is Chain-Owned Liquidity? How Katana Owns Its Liquidity

Chain-Owned Liquidity allows Katana to build and maintain liquidity that is owned by the network and deployed across its core DeFi markets. Rather than relying entirely on temporary liquidity incentives, Katana can use revenue generated by the network to acquire liquidity positions that remain part of the ecosystem over time. This gives Katana a way to continuously strengthen liquidity in important markets as network activity grows.

## **How Do Sequencer Fees Become Chain-Owned Liquidity?**

A portion of Katana's net sequencer fees can be used to build Chain-Owned Liquidity. These assets are deployed across core DeFi markets where additional liquidity can improve trading, lending, borrowing, and overall capital efficiency. Because the liquidity is owned by the network rather than rented from short-term liquidity providers, it can remain deployed and continue supporting Katana over time. As Katana generates more economic activity, additional revenue can be used to deepen these positions, creating a long-term source of liquidity for the ecosystem.

## **Core DeFi Markets**

Katana is designed around a set of core financial markets that provide the foundational primitives used throughout DeFi. These include:

- Spot trading through Katana's core spot DEX
- Lending and borrowing through Morpho
- Perpetual futures through Katana Perps

Applications built on Katana can use and compose with these shared markets rather than fragmenting liquidity across many separate venues. Chain-Owned Liquidity can be deployed where it is most useful to strengthen these markets and improve liquidity available to users and applications.

## **Productive Assets on Katana**

Katana also includes assets and infrastructure designed to generate additional economic value for the ecosystem. These include:

- Stablecoins such as AUSD
- Vault Bridge assets such as vbTokens
- Cross-chain assets brought to Katana through infrastructure such as Universal
- Bitcoin assets such as LBTC

Some of these assets can generate yield or other forms of protocol revenue. Alongside sequencer fees and activity generated by Katana's DeFi markets, these revenue sources can contribute to incentives, liquidity, and other mechanisms designed to make capital on Katana more productive.

## **Why Chain-Owned Liquidity Matters**

Liquidity incentives are effective for attracting capital, but that liquidity can leave when the incentives stop. Chain-Owned Liquidity gives Katana a complementary approach: instead of using all network revenue for temporary incentives, Katana can use part of that revenue to accumulate liquidity that the ecosystem continues to own.

Over time, this can create a compounding effect: **network activity generates revenue → revenue builds Chain-Owned Liquidity → deeper liquidity improves DeFi markets → stronger markets can generate more activity**. The goal is to build liquidity that becomes part of Katana's infrastructure rather than something the network continually has to rent.
